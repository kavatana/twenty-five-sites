Museum of Vanished Sounds

RELIC

Est. for sounds the century stopped making

Six exhibits. Six machines whose voices went quiet — not lost, just no longer needed. Nothing here was recorded; every sound on this floor is reconstructed live, from oscillators and filtered noise, the instant you press Listen.

IRotary Dial Telephone

Acc. 1985.06.T · Room I

Rotary Dial Telephone

In service 1892 – 1985

Ten numbered holes and a spring that owed you nothing. A full number took eleven seconds if your fingers were quick, twenty if someone in your life had a nine in it.

Reassembled from: filtered noise clicks, decelerating by hand.

Live reconstruction

IITypewriter & Carriage Bell

Acc. 1986.03.T · Room II

Typewriter, Carriage Return Bell

In service 1874 – 1986

Every letter struck the page with identical force, whichever you meant more. The bell at the margin was not a suggestion — it was the machine's only opinion.

Reassembled from: noise clacks, a sliding sweep, one sine bell.

Live reconstruction

IIIDial-Up Modem Handshake

Acc. 2006.14.T · Room III

Dial-Up Modem Handshake

In service 1958 – 2006

Before two modems spoke, they introduced themselves in six frequencies, apologised twice, and agreed on a shared language — a ritual repeated, unedited, on every single call.

Reassembled from: swept oscillators and noise, unabridged choreography.

Live reconstruction

IVCathode-Ray Degauss & Hum

Acc. 2015.02.T · Room IV

Cathode-Ray Degauss & Hum

In service 1938 – 2015

The picture tube's colour needed periodic, deliberate violence to remember its manners. The thunk was a magnet forgiving itself; the hum underneath never really stopped.

Reassembled from: a falling sine, two 60 Hz ghosts.

Live reconstruction

VFilm Projector Flutter

Acc. 1998.29.T · Room V

Film Projector Flutter

In service 1896 – 1998

Twenty-four still pictures a second, each one a small lie the eye agreed to believe. The clatter was proof of work; silence meant the reel, or the room, had ended.

Reassembled from: a noise loop clocked to 24 fps.

Live reconstruction

VITelegraph Key — ‘SOS’

Acc. 1999.01.T · Room VI

Telegraph Key — ‘SOS’

In service 1844 – 1999

Three short, three long, three short — the one sentence every operator on Earth was obliged to drop everything and answer. Commercial Morse fell silent on 1 February 1999.

Reassembled from: one gated tone, timed to international law.

Live reconstruction
