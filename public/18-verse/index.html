Verse — kinetic poetry

Words, held loosely.

Four short poems about the things that don’t stay — an orchard, an attention span, a sky, a Tuesday. Nothing on this page has agreed to hold still. Touch it and find out.

I
I Gravity

Windfall

The apple hangs by habit, not permission.

The branch was never asked to keep it.

One evening in October, habit ends —

and what we called forever

lands quietly in the grass.

Click a word. Gravity remembers it.

II
II Magnet

Nearness

Come closer and the sentence flinches,

the way a name thins when it’s said too often,

the way a coastline edits itself around every tide.

To read this closely is to change it.

Nothing stays the shape you found it in.

Reading, here, is a kind of touching.

III
III Assembly

Constellation

Someone once looked up at scattered light

and called it a bear, a hunter, a spoon —

gave the accidental a name, and a story.

The stars never agreed to the shape.

They only happened to be seen, once, together.

Scroll. Let it arrive.

IV
IV Erosion

Palimpsest

I meant to remember the ordinary Tuesday,

light on the table, a radio half-heard,

your hand, doing nothing in particular.

now I keep only that I meant to.

that, and your hand. everything else, water.

Keep reading. Watch what survives.
