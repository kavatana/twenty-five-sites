Wiring a street
out of divs九龍夜市

THE CONCEPT

Kowloon Nights is a love letter to the wet-neon alley — Wong Kar-wai glow, humming transformers, broth that outlived the lease. The brief to myself: build an entire isometric night-market street with zero images and zero WebGL. Every building, window, sign and puddle is a transformed <div>; the only pixels pushed by script are the rain.

THE STREET · CSS 3D

The ground is a 1600×1600 plane under rotateX(60deg) rotateZ(-45deg) — the classic 2:1 isometric camera — with transform-style: preserve-3d the whole way down. Each building is three faces: a roof at translateZ(h), and two walls stood up with rotateX(90deg) and rotateZ(90deg) rotateX(90deg) around a transform-origin on their ground edge. JavaScript generates the block from a seeded PRNG, so the street is random but identical on every load — useful when you're screen-recording.

The lit windows are CSS Grid cells inside each wall; roughly one in seven gets a steps(1,end) opacity animation with a negative random delay, so the block never blinks in unison. The neon signs use the inverse-camera trick: rotateZ(45deg) rotateX(-60deg) cancels the world rotation, billboarding each sign toward the viewer while it still sits — and parallaxes — at a true 3D coordinate above the street.

THE WEATHER · CANVAS + BLENDS

Rain is a single 2D canvas: 150 slanted strokes with depth-graded speed, length and alpha. Each drop carries its own floor — a random landing line in the lower third — and dies into an expanding elliptical splash, which fakes ground depth for free. Puddles are translucent radial gradients lying flat on the ground plane, and each sign drips a blurred gradient streak onto the asphalt as its reflection. They were mix-blend-mode: screen once — until we learned that a single blending child inside a preserve-3d context makes Chromium flatten the entire scene. The whole street collapsed into the asphalt. Alpha only, now. Steam is five blurred blobs on a staggered rise loop. DPR is capped at 2, and the whole loop idles when the tab hides or the hero scrolls away.

THE DYING LETTER

Authentic neon never fails politely. The buzzing letters (the masthead's final N, Blue Orchid's R) run a hand-tuned steps(1,end) keyframe track with irregular gaps — three bursts of stutter inside a 3.7s loop reads as electrical fault, not animation. Under prefers-reduced-motion, every flicker locks on, the camera parks, and the rain renders one static frame.

THE PASSES

Pass one raised the block and got the face math honest. Pass two was set dressing: reflections under every sign, cables with lantern dots, the noodle-stall glow pool. The last pass was composition against real screenshots — reseating the camera origin, rebalancing sign heights, and scaling the whole world to 0.52 so the street still reads on a phone held in one hand under an awning, which is the only correct way to browse it.
