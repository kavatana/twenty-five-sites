Room II · The Standing Collection

Six editions the curators refused to destroy.

Every re-mint erases a painting. These six were pardoned — seeds pinned under glass so the field repaints them, stroke for stroke, exactly as first shown. Walk the wall; each plaque names its palette.

Process · Wall Text

How the field paints.

There is no brush and no image to copy. There is only a field of directions, a crowd of particles, and time.

The field is laid down

A seed — one integer — feeds mulberry32, and the numbers it breathes out build a lattice of fractal value noise. Sampled anywhere on the canvas, the noise returns an angle. The whole painting-to-be is already present here, invisible, like grain in uncut stone.

The particles walk

A few thousand particles drop onto the canvas and do the only thing they know: ask the field which way, step, ask again. Each step leaves a stroke of near-transparent pigment. None of them knows about composition. The field knows.

The sediment settles

Strokes accumulate like silt — thousands of layers a second at first, then slower, thinner, quieter. Color is geological: each particle is born into a stratum of its palette and carries that ink for life. When the surface reads as stone, the edition is on view.

A painting you can regenerate is not a copy of anything. It is a place the mathematics has agreed to hold still.
— from the founding deed of the gallery, 2026
